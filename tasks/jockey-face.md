# Jockey Face — plan

A bright, realistic 3D horse race, with the riders galloping straight at you, at **theluckystrike.co.uk/jockey-face**.
You type your name, snap a selfie or pick a photo, and your face goes onto a big-headed
jockey. Add your friends' faces too, and they become the jockeys you race against. Then you
tap to race the field to the line.

Inspiration: the mobile racer screenshot (start gate, packed grandstand, a timer, a position
badge, a progress bar, SPRINT/JUMP buttons). We keep the HUD ideas, the bright festival-day
colours and the 3D, and make it chunky and old-school, like an N64/PS1 game.

## How the face mapping works

1. **Detect** the face with MediaPipe. If there's no face, ask for another photo. If there are several, use the biggest.
2. **Crop** a square around the face box with ~15% padding, then rotate so the eyes are level (from the eye keypoints).
3. **Stylise**: downscale to a 64×64 texture, lift contrast and saturation a little, and posterise lightly so the photo matches the flat-shaded world instead of looking pasted on.
4. **Map** it onto the jockey's head: the head mesh is UV-mapped so the front of the face is a clean square, with a soft-edged mask that blends into the skin-coloured head. The helmet and goggles strap sit on top as their own mesh.
5. **Make expression textures** from the one photo, using the keypoints to know where the eyes and mouth are:
   - *Normal* — the stylised face.
   - *Effort* — eyes squashed, mouth pushed open.
   - *Panic* — eyes widened, plus a 3D sweat-drop sprite.
   - *Joy* — mouth curved up, rosy cheeks.
   - *Dizzy* — swirly eye overlay and orbiting stars (after a stumble).
6. **Cache** the expressions as one small texture atlas per jockey, so switching an expression just moves the UV offset.

The jockeys are **bobbleheads**: the head is roughly double normal size so the face reads
from the side camera. The head is turned three-quarters towards the camera and sits on a
spring, so it lags and wobbles with each gallop stride, nods on landings and snaps back on a
stumble. That wobble is most of the charm.

Before building on it, try 10 varied photos (skin tones, glasses, beards, hats, low light, a
pet) and tune the stylising so every face stays recognisable. That's Phase 0.

## Your stable (v1)

Your stable is the set of faces saved on this device: you plus up to 5 friends.

- **Add a friend**: name + photo, same steps as your own jockey. Silks, horse and personality are filled in at random and can be changed.
- **Race lineup**: before each race you pick who rides. Empty spots are filled by CPU jockeys with drawn faces and silly names.
- Friends in your stable are raced by the computer. Each one gets a racing style (fast starter, closer, steady) that you can set or leave random.
- **Pass-and-play** stays a later idea; v1 is one player against the stable.
- The stable is local only. Nothing is shared unless you share a photo-finish card yourself.

## Jockey characteristics

Picked after the photo, each with a default so you can skip:

- **Silks** — 8 bright colour pairs × 4 patterns (stripes, hoops, star, checks), as texture swaps on the jersey and cap.
- **Horse** — name (random funny suggestions, editable) and coat (bay, chestnut, grey, black, palomino).
- **Personality** — sets the speech bubbles and a small gameplay twist:

  | Personality | Says things like | Twist |
  |---|---|---|
  | Showboat | "Watch this!" / "Too easy!" | Bigger bonus for perfect taps, bigger penalty for misses |
  | Cool Cucumber | "Steady…" / "Nice." | Stamina drains slower, smaller burst |
  | Hothead | "MOVE!" / "Come ON!" | Faster top speed, horse gets flustered sooner |
  | Lucky | "Hehe." / "Whoops—fine!" | One free save from a stumble per race |

- **Name tag** — the jockey's name (max 10 characters, letters, numbers and spaces only) on a plate that floats under the horse in their silk colour. Yours has a ▼ marker so you can always find yourself in the pack.

## Gameplay

**Goal:** finish first in a ~40-second race against 5 rivals (your stable, then CPU jockeys).

**One control: tap anywhere** (Space or Enter on desktop). Tapping as fast as you can isn't
the best way to win, so the race has rhythm:

- **Stride rhythm** — every gallop stride has a beat (hoofbeat sound, plus a pulse ring on the ground under the horse). A tap on the beat is a *Perfect* (big push, Effort face). Close is *Good*. A random tap gives a small push and costs extra stamina.
- **Stamina** — a bar under your name tag. Mashing drains it fast; tapping on the beat drains it slowly. At empty, the horse slows to a canter and your face goes to Panic until it recovers.
- **Hurdles** (2–3 per race) — a marker flashes when one is coming. Tap inside the jump window and you clear it; too early or late is a stumble (Dizzy face, big slowdown). The same tap does the jump, so there's still only one control.
- **Home stretch** — the last 15%. The camera pulls in, the crowd roars, the beat speeds up, and stamina stops mattering: mash as hard as you can. This is where races are won.
- **Starting gate** — a 3-2-1 countdown. Tapping on "GO" exactly gives a jump start; tapping before it is a false start (you're held back a second).

**Rivals:** pace profiles (fast starter, closer, steady) and light rubber-banding keep the
pack close so the finish is tense. Three difficulty levels: Maiden, Handicap, Derby.

**Head-on view:** hurdles come into view at the bottom of the screen as the camera passes
them, then slide back towards the pack. That gives you time to see the jump coming before
your horse reaches it.

**Screen layout (landscape, with a "rotate your phone" prompt in portrait):**
- Top left: race clock. Top centre: progress bar with each jockey's face as a dot. Top right: pause.
- Position badge ("1ST", "3RD") under the clock, with the gap in lengths.
- Scene (looking back down the track): sky and clouds, low-poly hills, trees on one side, a grandstand with an instanced cheering crowd and bunting on the other, a big screen showing a live close-up of *your face* (a second camera rendering to a texture), white rails, dirt track with lane lines.

**Finish:** a slow-motion freeze as noses cross the line, then a **photo-finish** card: the
finishing frame in sepia with the bobbleheads, your name, place, time and best time. Buttons:
Race again, Change lineup, Share (Web Share API with the card as a PNG, download as fallback).

**Round 15 (2026-11-02), real race, leaderboard and The Hetty Cup:**
- `race.js`: states attract → countdown → racing → finished. Race is **400 m**. Your speed comes only from taps: the tap rate over the last second sets a target speed, `19 × (1 − e^(−rate/4.5))` m/s (8 taps/s ≈ 16 m/s, so mashing has a ceiling), and the horse eases towards it. A tap before GO is a **false start**: held in the gate for 1 s, panic face. CPU riders have a cruising speed (13.4–15.3 m/s), a surge rhythm, and some are closers (+1.3 m/s in the last 30%); light rubber-banding keeps the pack close (your time is unaffected). Exact finish times are interpolated within the step.
- `hud.js`: race clock, position (1ST…6TH), progress bar with a dot per horse (yours bigger and named) and a finish flag, a big "M TO GO" counter, a PACE meter for your tap rate, and call-outs at 300 / 200 / FINAL 100M.
- Track: distance boards (300, 200, 100, FINISH) on both rails, facing the oncoming field.
- Faces react: effort when you tap 6+/s, panic after a false start, joy for the winner.
- `leaderboard.js`: top 10 quickest times **on this device** (localStorage), shown on the results screen (your entry flashes) and alternating with the logo on the title screen. A shared online board needs a small server (e.g. Vercel + Upstash Redis from the Marketplace) and name moderation: decide in Phase 1.
- `cup.js`: **The Hetty Cup**, a gold trophy built in code (turned bowl and stem, loop handles, black plinth with an engraved plate: "THE HETTY CUP / winner / time"), spinning in its own renderer on the results screen. Win and the screen reads "<NAME> WINS THE HETTY CUP" with your time; lose and it says who took the cup and in what time. RACE AGAIN or NEW PLAYER.
- Played in the browser with simulated taps: 8 taps/s won in 26.24 s; 4 taps/s came 6th in 36.79 s while a CPU won in 28.11 s.

**Carry into the build:** short-range face model only finds faces that fill a fair part of the photo; add a manual crop fallback for group or distant shots. Name tags pile up when horses bunch at the start; stagger or hide rivals' tags.
