ALL CAPS: A room for MF DOOM, with 19 secrets and all 77 Special Herbs

Four days, about 135 messages, and five different DOOMs before the right one. What worked, what I threw away, and how I prompted.

Mark JenkinsNovember 202613 min readOpen the room ↗

The cover of MM..FOOD is a man in a metal mask eating cereal. I wanted to walk into that painting, sit him down and pour the milk. So I asked Claude to build it.

01The idea

My Listening Room has a neon sign on the wall with a DOOM lyric on it. That sign was a nod to him. This project is a whole room for him.

Daniel Dumile died in 2020. He left behind a body of work that rewards you for paying attention: every album is a different character, every beat on Special Herbs is named after a herb or a spice, and you have to write his name in capitals or you've spelled it wrong.

A list of facts about him would miss the point. He hid things. So the tribute had to be a place you poke around in.

ALL CAPS is a room you walk around in your browser, as DOOM. There are six things to do, nineteen secrets to find, and 77 records in the crates. All the music is real: 30-second previews from Deezer and Apple Music.

02The brief

My first message set the idea and the order of work. The last five lines were these:

First research
Then help me plan
Then design
Then build
Then deploy

That's a normal project plan, and Claude followed it. It came back with research on the cover and on the man, a plan, three questions (new repo or existing, first or third person, one corner or the full room), and then it built.

An hour after the first message there was a room on a live URL, with a blocky DOOM made of code who could eat cereal. The other sixteen hours went on making it good.

03What got built

17hfirst message to first version
~135messages from me, over four days
30pull requests
5DOOMs before the right one

What you can do in the room:

  • Eat breakfast. He sits facing you, cereal on one side and milk on the other. Pour each with its own hand, pick up the spoon and eat. Watch for the last spoonful. It's the cover, turned round.
  • Cook. Every dish is a track title from MM..FOOD. Pick one and find its three ingredients.
  • Make a beat. Sixteen pads and a step sequencer, swung at 88 BPM.
  • Dig the crates. All 77 Special Herbs instrumentals across six crates. Pull one and it plays. There's a counter for how many you've dug, and one record at the back of the last crate has no name.
  • Watch cartoons. Sit on the sofa and flip channels.
  • Finish the rhymes. A notebook with the last word missing from each line.
  • Find the secrets. Nineteen of them: a wardrobe, a telescope on the balcony, a calendar with the wrong birthday, a milk carton asking if you've seen someone.

04The look: I polished the wrong thing for hours

This is the part I'd do differently, so it gets the most words.

The first version looked like a cartoon: flat colours, black outlines, a character built from boxes and cylinders. It worked, and it wasn't what I wanted. I asked for something else:

I want to explore a hand drawn but more realistic graphic style and art direction. Help me to do that

Claude built a set of styles I could flip between: pencil, ink, gouache, ink with a colour wash. I picked ink and gouache. Then I spent the afternoon asking for more detail, better hands, more lifelike movement, a better mask. Each round was better than the last, and it still wasn't what I had in my head.

Around hour eight I said what I should have said at hour two:

Im wondering how we go more photo realistic here, we're not making a stepped improvement

And a few minutes later, looking at yet another round on his hat:

the hat is off, but we should stop and improve with photorealism instead

That message changed the project more than any other. Claude switched to real materials, light from a photographed room, and furniture scanned from real objects. The drawn styles are gone from the final thing.

That one is on me. I was giving good small notes on a direction I hadn't questioned, and Claude will keep improving whatever is in front of it. Asking "is this the right thing?" was my job, and I asked it six hours late.

05Five DOOMs

One: built from code. He was boxes and cylinders with a mask made of plates, which was fine for a cartoon. When I asked for realism, Claude proposed sculpting him better in code. I pushed back:

What level of realism are you proposing? It feels like you need to do some research into him first

It went away and researched him properly: build, clothes by era, how the mask sits, how he stood. I sent three reference photos.

Two: a generic human. My next suggestion was to stop building and start finding:

What if we search for models and use those instead. If you're missing a model then build one from scratch.

Claude built a body from open-source human data and dressed it. It was better, but he still looked like a mannequin.

Three: a scanned person. I found a 3D scan of a real man on Sketchfab and asked if it could use that, add a mask, and change the build. Claude asked the right question back (it can only download with my account), so I uploaded the file. It kept the scan's body and clothes, turned the purple hoodie green, removed the head and fitted its own, with a mask model I found separately.

One decision I didn't make and agree with: Claude wouldn't recolour the scanned man's face to turn him into someone else. It removed the head and built a new one. It also painted the brand logos off the hoodie.

Four: less. The last version came from taking things away.

I think we can drop the gilet completely, it looks a mess?

Remove the baseball cap too, it looks like trash, go back to the bald head

That left a hoodie, jeans and the mask. I thought that was him when I first published this.

Five: bigger. The morning after, he still looked like a man of average build in a hoodie. I asked:

Do some further research into the body shape and size of mf doom: if he needs to be bigger, make it so

He needed to be bigger. Nobody published his height or weight, but the people who met him wrote down what they saw. The Wire, in 2005: "His perfectly round belly juts from under a shirt that, surprisingly, isn't baggy enough to obscure it." And when stand-ins started turning up at his shows, one of the ways fans caught them was that the man on stage was "much thinner".

So DOOM got a round belly, a thicker chest, neck and arms. The Doomposter in the wardrobe kept the old, slimmer body, so the thinner one is the fake.

06How it works

The short version. Skip it if you came for the prompting.

  • The site: plain HTML, CSS and three.js. No framework and no build step. Hosted on Vercel and served at theluckystrike.co.uk/doom.
  • The light: a photographed room (an HDRI) lights everything, with soft contact shadows and a little bloom added after each frame. On a slow device it drops to a lighter picture by itself.
  • The furniture: 21 scanned models from Poly Haven, all free to use. Claude wrote a script that downloads them, shrinks them and places them.
  • The walking: the scan came with one animation, a dance. The walk is worked out live: where each foot should land, then the knee and hip angles to get it there. That's why his feet stay planted and don't slide.
  • The music: a small server function turns a track into a fresh 30-second preview from Deezer, with Apple Music as backup. Deezer's links die after about 15 minutes, so each one is fetched when you need it.
  • The footsteps: synthesised in the browser, a different sound for wood, rug and balcony.

07How I prompted

The same way as last time: like a design crit. These are my real messages.

Report what you see

After eating breakfast he gets stuck on the chair

his feet also sink into the ground, looks weird

Still messy, wire is half broken, starts from the middle of the floor

I didn't guess at causes or suggest fixes, and I often attached a screenshot. Claude found the cause each time, and usually a second bug next to it.

Say when it feels cheap

on the loading screen, remove the jump when the loader has finished and the button appears. that makes it look cheap

the rain needs to be faster, it feels like it lags a bit

"Cheap" and "lags" are feelings. Both got turned into measurements: the layout shift went to zero pixels, the intro animation went to 0.8 seconds.

Question the words

Back into the room is a weird CTA, why is it that

Claude had made the button say something different for returning visitors. It explained why, I said no, and it's now "Enter the room" for everyone.

Ask it a question back

I'd forgotten two of the nineteen secrets and asked for the list, and for a way to hide that list in the interface. Claude proposed a counter you can click. I asked:

would villain give you cryptic hints for the ones that are left?

So that's in. Click the secrets counter and you see blanks. Type villain and each blank gets a clue.

Think about day two

Need to add much more tracks in the crates, that builds longevity. Put in all the special herbs

The crates had seven records. They now have 77, with a counter. It's the cheapest reason to come back that I could think of.

Go back for the weakest part

A day after publishing, the breakfast still bothered me. It's the cover, and it was the worst thing in the room: the cereal just appeared in the bowl, and he never touched the box. I asked:

How feasible is to really improve the eating of the cereal, including the pouring of the milk. This is the weakest part

Very, Claude said, and listed what it would build. Then I changed the staging to make the job easier:

Why don't we go with that approach but make this easier. There than having him sit at the table and camera behind like the first image shared which kicked all of this off, let's instead show him facing the camera, cereal on left and milk on right. And he uses each hand to pour cereal or milk into the bowl.

Facing the camera puts the mask in shot, and it hides the inside of the bowl until the last spoonful, when the camera cuts to straight above it.

This was the first thing I reviewed as video. Claude can't watch its own animation at speed, so it stepped the scene one frame at a time and sent me clips. It took seven.

He doesn't pick up the carton or the box, they float. The spoon is not in his hand and looks weird

Are the box and carton too big too?

Overall better. Hands are still passing through the edges of the box and carton, looks odd

They were too big; the bowl was 35 cm across. And his hands kept going through things until Claude stopped judging by eye and wrote a check that measured, on every frame, how far any part of his hand was inside the box. That check found problems neither of us had named, including that he couldn't reach the cereal at all.

Once it moved properly I zoomed in. Four cropped screenshots, a few words each:

Spoon and hand needs work

Hand sinking in to the table

beard and mask is problematic

The mask should go over the beard

The first one caught a bug no video had. His grip on the spoon only existed while a spoonful was in progress. Between spoonfuls his hand went slack, and the thumb I'd asked him to fold in that morning had never shown in that state. A still frame at the right moment found what seven clips hadn't.

09On a phone

The first phone layout put the music player on top of the joystick. I sent a screenshot. The fix worked and the next screenshot showed a different clash, so I stopped sending clashes:

it all needs a better layout on a mobile device. rethink the system for it, the cards the toasts and the controls. so they don't clash but are workable and make sense

This time Claude came back with a set of rules. The top of the screen is for things the room tells you: progress, what's playing, a lyric. The bottom is for your thumbs: joystick left, one green button right, with its label sitting on top of it. Cards and panels take the whole screen and put the controls away.

Then I turned the phone sideways and suggested moving the player to the gap between the joystick and the button. I asked Claude to test my idea against its own system. It agreed, for a reason: sideways, height is what you're short of, and that gap is the only free space.

Two days later I played it sideways on my own phone and it was still crowded. A lyric sat over his face, the player sat over his bowl, and a strip down the left was empty.

Let's fix up landscape and portrait HUD layout. There's wasted space on the left on landscape and it's very busy. Think about it from a systems perspective and fix it

The answer was one rule. Everything that floats over the room is one of three kinds, and each kind has one home: status along the top, notices never over the middle of the picture, controls under your thumbs. Upright, notices stack under the top. Sideways, the screen becomes three columns: notices left, him in the middle, actions right. The player shrank to one slim line, and the joystick goes away when he sits down, because there's nowhere to walk.

10What I took from it

Question the direction early. I lost hours improving a style I should have dropped. The tool won't tell you that you're polishing the wrong thing. Ask yourself every hour or so.

Find, don't build. The character got good when we stopped sculpting and started from a scan of a real person. Same with the furniture and the mask. Most of what you need exists and is free to use, with credit.

Taking away is the fastest fix. The gilet, the cap and five art styles all went. Each removal made it better in one message.

Ask for a system when you've sent three screenshots. By the third clash the real problem was a missing rule, and fixing them one at a time was never going to supply it.

Watch it move. I judged everything from screenshots until the breakfast scene. Movement needs video, and it needs measuring: the faults I could see in the clips were all there in the numbers, along with some I couldn't.

Details matter. That was my last message before bed, with a list of eight small things. The spoon stopped ten centimetres short of the bowl. His hands were a shade lighter than his face. Most visitors couldn't have named either problem, but I think they'd have sensed something was off. Both were fixed by morning.

Go and have breakfast with him. Dig a few herbs out of the crates. And remember: all caps when you spell the man's name.
